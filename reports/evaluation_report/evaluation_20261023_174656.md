- Conversations are heterogeneous: many are benign (positive feedback, short FAQs) while a few are high-risk (escalations, billing, refunds, admissions, repeated rephrasing). Pure random sampling can miss or under-sample these high-risk cases.
- Signals (thumbs-up/thumbs-down, abrupt ends, escalations, re-asks) are useful proxies to prioritize where hallucinations are likeliest.
- You can oversample rare but important strata to get enough examples to evaluate, then weight results back to a population-level hallucination rate.

Practical stratified plan (example, keep total evaluator budget = 1,000):
- Baseline random sample: 200 — gives an unbiased overall check and baseline hallucination estimate.
- Thumbs-down / user complaints / escalations: 350 — likely to contain most hallucinations; oversample heavily.
- Topic-sensitive conversations (money, refunds, admissions, legal/medical if present): 200 — critical topics need more coverage.
- Abrupt session ends / many rephrases / repeated clarifying questions: 150 — signals of confusion or model error.
- Thumbs-up / clearly satisfied: 100 — small sample to validate the assumption that thumbs-up correlates with low hallucination.

How to use results:
- Have the LLM evaluator label each sample per your rubric (hallucination yes/no, severity, type).
- Compute per-stratum hallucination rates, then compute a weighted overall hallucination rate using the true proportions of each stratum in the 50k population.
- Use per-stratum rates to create targeted alerts and remediation (e.g., retrain for money/refunds, tune flows that lead to abrupt endings).

- Ignoring all thumbs-up conversations entirely is risky; thumbs-up is a good signal but not perfect—keep a small sample to validate that assumption.
